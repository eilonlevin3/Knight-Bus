Purpose

What we decide today

The problem

The owner reports the ghost "works not as well as expected". The ghost is the dashed 👻 marker that moves a bus forward from its last signal, and its timer counts down to the user's stop. If it is wrong, the timer is wrong, and people miss buses.

Decision requested

  • Which improvement to build first.
  • Whether to accept a possible running cost (about US$5 a month) for the option that needs it.
  • Whether to keep the accuracy test as a permanent quality check.
Bottom line up front: the ghost's model is already 3–4× better than no ghost. Most of what feels wrong is how it behaves on screen (it jumps about once a minute and gives a single exact-looking number). The model's error only matters in rush hour. Fix the behaviour first, then add live traffic.

How it works now · 2 of 2

The model: dead reckoning along the route

age = now − time of the bus's last signal (capped at 30 min) position = last signal, snapped onto the route's path (no ghost if > 250 m off) ghost(now) = walk forward along the path, spending `age` seconds, each stretch taking T(stretch, half-hour, weekday|weekend) timer = time for the ghost to reach the selected stop, by the same T

Stretch time T, in order

  1. Last month's mean for this half hour
  2. Same stretch, whole day
  3. Timetable
  4. 18 km/h

Redraw

Twice a second (500 ms). It moves evenly within a stretch, so it doesn't model the stop dwell itself.

New signal

The ghost restarts from the new signal, wherever that is. Whatever it got wrong shows up as a sudden jump.

Code: src/liveLines.js (ghostOf, drawGhosts, ghostSecondsTo), src/lib/routeGeometry.js (advanceOnPath, travelSeconds), pipeline/build-speeds.mjs.

Method

How we measured: a replay of real buses

The test

For every recorded bus signal, the ghost predicts where the bus will be after 1, 2, 3, 5 and 8 minutes. We compare that with where the same bus's later signal really put it. The error is turned into seconds of driving, which is how wrong the timer would be.

  • The same code as the app (advanceOnPath, the real speeds files).
  • The ideas are run on the same signals, side by side.
  • Terminals left out (buses wait there).

Data: Open Bus per-minute files for Sunday 2026-10-18 (a working day in Israel), all four metro areas. Two windows: rush hour 08:00–09:30 and midday 12:00–13:00.

Diagnosis

Why it still feels wrong: four findings

Idea 1 of 3

Live stretch times from all buses

Use what buses on the same stretch actually took in the last 20 minutes, from any route, blended with the monthly history. The more buses we have, the more weight the live times get (weight = n ÷ (n + 4)).

What it fixes

Today's traffic: the rush-hour error where the ghost runs ahead. Where there is no live data, it is the same as today.

What it costs

  • The relay has to follow every bus from minute to minute and remember the last 20 minutes. Today it deliberately does not read the whole file, because a free Worker has only 10 ms of CPU per run.
  • Most likely Cloudflare Workers Paid (about US$5 a month) for a scheduled job once a minute, plus a store for state between minutes. To be confirmed.
  • A free variant on the phone itself (only buses of the routes on screen) covers less and needs about 20 minutes to warm up.
  • Effort: 3–5 days. Risk: medium (a new moving part in the backend).
Idea 2 of 3

This bus's own pace

If this bus has driven slower than history over its last 3–10 minutes, assume it keeps doing so: scale its stretch times by its own ratio (clamped to 0.5–2, applied at 60%).

Why it looked attractive

  • Runs entirely on the phone. No backend, no cost.
  • Works for 90% of signals (needs 3+ minutes of history for the bus).
  • Effort: 1 day.

What we measured

It is worse. A bus's last few minutes say more about the lights and stops it just passed than about the road ahead. The scaling adds noise, and only cancels the bias by chance.

Idea 3 of 3

Glide, and say how sure we are

Keep the model. Change what the user sees when the model is corrected, and how certain the timer looks.

1 · Glide, don't jump

When a new signal arrives, the ghost slides to the corrected place over about 4 s instead of jumping (today the jump is most of the time). It never moves backwards past the real bus marker.

2 · An honest timer

The timer shows ≈ 3:40 plus a range taken from this test, by signal age. The range is the one that held 90% of cases: in rush hour and at midday when the signal is 2 min old. When the range is wider than the time itself, it shows "due" instead of seconds.

3 · Fade with age

The ghost fades as its signal gets older and turns into an outline after 8 minutes (90% of errors there are over 3½ minutes). Today it runs on for up to 30.

Cost and risk

Phone only, no running cost, no new data. Effort: 1–2 days. Risk: low. It doesn't make the estimate more accurate, it makes the error visible and smooth. The jumps are the most visible flaw the owner described ("updating and moving every few seconds" was the redraw rate, now fixed; the jump once a minute remains).

Recommendation

Build idea 3 now, idea 1 next, drop idea 2

Phase 1 · now

Idea 3: glide and confidence

Cheapest, no risk, and it targets what the user notices: the jump once a minute and a timer that looks exact. 1–2 days.

Phase 2 · after a cost check

Idea 1: live stretch times

The only idea that makes the model itself better, and only where it is weak (rush hour, about 5–9%). First confirm the Workers cost; if it isn't acceptable, ship the free phone-only variant.

Rejected

Idea 2: own pace

Measured worse in both windows. It isn't worth building.

Also: keep the measuring stick

Add this replay to the repository as pipeline/backtest-ghost.mjs and run it weekly. It is how we will know if a change helps, and it catches a bad month of history (see the rush-hour bias). Success criteria for phase 1: no visible jump over 30 m on screen; the timer's range holds the real arrival at least 90% of the time.

Limits of this review

Caveats and open questions

Caveats

  • One day, two windows. The rush-hour bias may come from the history window: the last 30 days include the Sukkot holiday weeks, when traffic is light. To check on a normal month.
  • The speeds files used were built with this day inside their 30 days (1 day in 30), which slightly flatters today's ghost.
  • "Truth" is the bus's own later signal, which has GPS noise of its own (tens of metres).
  • Errors are in seconds of driving at usual speed, not measured arrival times at the stop.

Open questions for the review

  • Is about US$5 a month acceptable for live traffic in the ghost?
  • Should the timer show a range, or a single number that turns into "due"?
  • Should history drop holiday days, or use the median instead of the mean? (Needs a pipeline change to test.)
  • Light rail and trains: the same model, not measured separately here.
